# Structural Patterns: 28 Before/After Examples

This is the complete named taxonomy. Signals prompt contextual stylistic review, not proof of authorship. Examples illustrate editing choices, not verified research; retained claims still require appropriate support. Preserve meaning, certainty, emphasis, technical terms, and the author's voice. Editorial notes are not publishable prose; when a substantive change needs evidence or author approval, flag it separately rather than inventing a replacement.

## Category 1: Content Patterns (6)

### Pattern 1: Inflated Significance
**Signal:** Treating mundane topics as world-changing

**Before:**
> "Email is one of the most revolutionary technologies ever created. It fundamentally changed how humanity communicates across distances."

**After:**
> "Email is one of the most revolutionary technologies ever created. It fundamentally changed long-distance communication."

**Editorial note:** The superlative and scale of change need support. Ask for evidence or permission to narrow them; do not invent a history of earlier communication methods.

---

### Pattern 2: Unwarranted Notability Claims
**Signal:** "Fascinating", "curious phenomenon", "a fascinating question"

**Before:**
> "A fascinating aspect of modern work culture is the rise of remote teams. This curious phenomenon has captured the attention of researchers worldwide."

**After:**
> "Remote teams are on the rise in modern work culture and have attracted researchers' attention worldwide."

**Editorial note:** Verify the rise and worldwide research interest; removing evaluative decoration does not substantiate either claim.

---

### Pattern 3: Superficial Analysis
**Signal:** Listing points without depth, stakes, or contradiction

**Before:**
> "There are several reasons to adopt new technologies. First, they increase efficiency. Second, they reduce costs. Third, they improve user experience."

**After:**
> "New technologies increase efficiency, reduce costs, and improve the user experience."

**Editorial note:** Which benefit matters here, and what evidence supports it? Ask for concrete stakes or evidence rather than inventing details or weakening the claim without approval.

---

### Pattern 4: Promotional Language
**Signal:** Sales-speak masquerading as editorial or technical writing

**Before:**
> "This innovative solution empowers teams to unlock unprecedented value while driving meaningful transformation across the organization."

**After:** Deferred pending supported specifics.

**Editorial note:** Ask what the solution changes for teams and the organization. Without a supported benefit, retain the original pending clarification rather than inventing a time saving; this instruction is not revised prose.

---

### Pattern 5: Vague Attributions
**Signal:** No source for claims; "some say", "research shows", "experts agree"

**Before:**
> "Research suggests that remote work increases productivity by up to 40%. Experts agree that flexibility is crucial to employee satisfaction."

**Retained pending verification (not a substantive rewrite):**
> "Research suggests that remote work increases productivity by up to 40%. Experts agree that flexibility is crucial to employee satisfaction."

**Editorial note:** This passage needs verified sources before a substantive rewrite. Keep the claims pending verification; do not substitute an invented study, statistic, or personal experience.

---

### Pattern 6: Formulaic Future Section
**Signal:** Closing with "As technology evolves..." or "We can expect..."

**Before:**
> "The landscape of work continues to evolve. As technology advances, we can expect even greater integration of AI and automation in the workplace. The future will undoubtedly bring new challenges and opportunities."

**After:**
> "Work continues to evolve. As technology advances, we can expect even greater integration of AI and automation in the workplace. The future will undoubtedly bring new challenges and opportunities."

**Editorial note:** The prediction remains generic. Ask for supported specifics and confirmation of its certainty; do not invent uncertainty about timing or silently remove the prediction.

---

## Category 2: Language Patterns (7)

### Pattern 7: Inflated Vocabulary
**Signal:** "utilize" instead of "use", "facilitate" instead of "help"

**Before:**
> "We leverage cutting-edge methodologies to facilitate enhanced outcomes for our diverse stakeholder ecosystem."

**After:**
> "We use cutting-edge methods to help improve outcomes for our diverse stakeholder ecosystem."

**Editorial note:** Ask which methods and outcomes the author means and what supports "cutting-edge"; do not replace stakeholder relationships with generic claims.

---

### Pattern 8: Copula Avoidance
**Signal:** Rewriting to avoid "is": "The challenge: complexity" instead of "The challenge is complex"

**Before:**
> "The key objective: increased market penetration. The primary challenge: resource allocation constraints. The ultimate goal: sustainable growth."

**After:**
> "The key objective is increased market penetration. The primary challenge is resource allocation constraints. The ultimate goal is sustainable growth."

---

### Pattern 9: Negative Parallelism
**Signal:** "Not only... but also not..." constructions

**Before:**
> "This approach is not only inefficient but also fails to address the underlying constraints, not to mention its lack of scalability in distributed environments."

**After:**
> "This approach is inefficient, fails to address the underlying constraints, and lacks scalability in distributed environments."

---

### Pattern 10: Forced Rule-of-Three
**Signal:** Artificially grouping three items for rhythm, even when 2 or 4 is more natural

**Before:**
> "Successful teams need three things: clear communication, defined processes, and strong leadership."

**After:**
> "Successful teams need clear communication, defined processes, and strong leadership."

Remove the insistence on three without changing the author's priorities or discarding legitimate items.

---

### Pattern 11: Synonym Cycling
**Signal:** Using different words for the same concept in close proximity

**Before:**
> "The solution provides enhanced efficiency. This optimization improves productivity. The streamlined approach delivers increased performance benefits."

**After:**
> "The solution improves efficiency. This optimization improves productivity. The streamlined approach improves performance."

Retain efficiency, productivity, and performance when they denote distinct outcomes; do not collapse them just to avoid related vocabulary.

---

### Pattern 12: False Ranges
**Signal:** "Anywhere from X to Y" when you just mean "about X"

**Before:**
> "The project took anywhere from 3 to 9 months, depending on various factors."

**After:**
> "The project took 3 to 9 months, depending on the circumstances."

Preserve the supplied range unless a source supports a single estimate.

---

### Pattern 13: Hedge Clusters
**Signal:** Multiple qualifiers in one sentence

**Before:**
> "It could be argued that, in a sense, one might suggest that this approach arguably somewhat optimizes the process."

**After:**
> "It could be argued that this approach somewhat optimizes the process."

The revision retains a qualified assertion and limited degree. Confirm that the removed qualifiers are redundant; do not add a first-person admission of doubt.

---

## Category 3: Style Patterns (8)

### Pattern 14: Em Dash Overuse
**Signal:** Repeated em dashes interrupting prose or creating a mechanical rhythm

**Before:**
> "The challenge — which is multifaceted and nuanced — requires a comprehensive approach. This solution — developed through extensive research — addresses all key concerns."

**After:**
> "The multifaceted, nuanced challenge requires a comprehensive approach. This solution, developed through extensive research, addresses all key concerns."

---

### Pattern 15: Mechanical Boldface
**Signal:** Bolding topic words in every paragraph for "emphasis"

**Before:**
> "**Communication** is essential for team success. **Transparency** builds trust. **Accountability** drives results."

**After:**
> "Communication is essential for team success. Transparency builds trust. Accountability drives results."

---

### Pattern 16: Inline-Header Lists
**Signal:** "**First:** explanation. **Second:** explanation."

**Before:**
> "There are three key components. **First:** strategic planning enables goal alignment. **Second:** resource allocation optimizes deployment. **Third:** performance monitoring ensures accountability."

**After:**
> "There are three key components: strategic planning enables goal alignment; resource allocation optimizes deployment; performance monitoring ensures accountability."

Retain the numbered format instead when the sequence or scanning benefit matters.

---

### Pattern 17: Title-Case Headings
**Signal:** When sentence case is more natural for the context

**Before:**
> "# How To Implement A New System
> ## The Challenges Of Modern Software Development
> ### Understanding The Core Principles"

**After:**
> "# How to implement a new system
> ## The challenges of modern software development
> ### Understanding the core principles"

---

### Pattern 18: Emoji Decoration
**Signal:** 🚀 ✨ 💡 as filler, not information

**Before:**
> "Here are the key benefits: 🚀 Speed ✨ Quality 💡 Innovation 🎯 Results"

**After:**
> "Key benefits: speed, quality, innovation, results."

---

### Pattern 19: Curly Quotation Marks in Technical Contexts
**Signal:** Smart quotes in code, technical specs, or where straight quotes belong

**Before:**
> Use the “const” keyword to define constants.

**After:**
> Use the "const" keyword to define constants.

---

### Pattern 20: ALL CAPS for Emphasis
**Signal:** When italics or restructuring is better

**Before:**
> "This is CRITICALLY IMPORTANT to understand before proceeding."

**After:**
> "Understanding this is critically important before proceeding."

---

### Pattern 21: Bullet-List Defaults
**Signal:** Every list becomes bullets instead of prose or numbers

**Before:**
> "The process involves several steps:
> - First, gather all necessary information and requirements
> - Next, develop a comprehensive plan that addresses stakeholder needs
> - Then, execute the plan with regular monitoring
> - Finally, assess outcomes and iterate based on feedback"

**After:**
> "First, gather all necessary information and requirements. Next, develop a comprehensive plan that addresses stakeholder needs. Then, execute the plan with regular monitoring. Finally, assess outcomes and iterate based on feedback."

Keep the original list if it makes these steps easier to follow; prose is an option, not a requirement.

---

## Category 4: Performed Authenticity Patterns (7)

### Pattern 22: Philosophical Mic Drop
**Signal:** Ending with rhetorical flourish or question

**Before:**
> "In the end, perhaps the real question isn't whether AI will change the world—it's whether we're ready for it."

**After:**
> "Perhaps the real question is whether we're ready for AI to change the world, rather than whether it will."

The open question can remain. Do not turn it into an unsupported conclusion about readiness or uncertainty about AI's effects.

---

### Pattern 23: Perfectly Balanced Contrasts
**Signal:** "On one hand... on the other hand..." when symmetry substitutes for a substantive tradeoff

**Before:**
> "On one hand, remote work increases flexibility and improves work-life balance. On the other hand, it reduces spontaneous collaboration and can lead to isolation."

**After:**
> "Remote work increases flexibility and improves work-life balance, but it reduces spontaneous collaboration and can lead to isolation."

Keep the original balanced structure if it helps the argument. Do not invent a priority or remove either side of the tradeoff.

---

### Pattern 24: Brand Manifesto Structure
**Signal:** "We believe. We value. We are."

**Before:**
> "We believe in innovation. We value transparency. We are committed to excellence. We stand for integrity. We drive impact."

**After:**
> "We believe in innovation, value transparency, are committed to excellence, stand for integrity, and drive impact."

**Editorial note:** A brand statement may legitimately name values. Ask for supplied examples if needed; do not turn aspirations into invented operating practices. The impact claim still needs support.

---

### Pattern 25: Parenthetical Personality Injection
**Signal:** Forced humanity in asides

**Before:**
> "This process is, as I mentioned earlier, somewhat complicated (honestly, it's a mess). The solution (spoiler: it took three years) finally worked."

**After:**
> "This process is somewhat complicated; honestly, it's a mess. The solution finally worked after three years."

Retain the supplied aside's emphasis if it belongs to the author's voice; removing parentheses does not require removing personality or changing the timeline to a search for a solution.

---

### Pattern 26: Self-Aware Meta-Commentary
**Signal:** "As I mentioned earlier..." in single-turn or short writing

**Before:**
> "As I noted in the introduction, the challenge is multifaceted. As I explained above, the solution addresses core concerns."

**After:**
> "The challenge is multifaceted. The solution addresses core concerns."

---

### Pattern 27: Casual Code-Switching
**Signal:** Abrupt shifts from formal to slang within one piece

**Before:**
> "The implementation of distributed ledger technologies fundamentally transforms asset management, no cap. One could argue that blockchain is lowkey revolutionary, fam."

**After:**
> "The implementation of distributed ledger technologies fundamentally transforms asset management. One could argue that blockchain is somewhat revolutionary."

**Editorial note:** This removes the abrupt slang without replacing technical terms or adding judgments about usefulness. The scale of change still needs support; preserve the author's qualified second claim.

---

### Pattern 28: The "Relatable" Opener
**Signal:** "You've probably noticed..." or "We've all been there"

**Before:**
> "You've probably noticed that modern work is increasingly complex. We've all felt the pain of inefficient processes. Everyone knows that collaboration is harder than ever."

**After:**
> "Modern work is increasingly complex. Inefficient processes cause pain. Collaboration is harder than ever."

**Editorial note:** Removing presumed shared experience does not verify the underlying claims, including "harder than ever". Ask for support without inventing claims about tools.

---

## Leave-Unchanged Example

**Original and revised (unchanged):**
> "Evaluate three properties: latency, throughput, and fault tolerance. Replication improves availability, but it increases coordination costs."

The list names distinct technical criteria, and the balanced contrast states a substantive tradeoff. Neither needs a stylistic rewrite; retain the technical terms and equal weight without inventing a preference.

## Full Multi-Pattern Example

### Before (Multiple stylistic signals):
> "The landscape of remote work is fundamentally transforming how organizations facilitate employee engagement. Research suggests that distributed teams enhance innovation while optimizing operational efficiency—though one could argue that, in a sense, remote work presents unique challenges. As we navigate this evolving ecosystem, three critical factors emerge: streamlined communication, robust infrastructure, and adaptive leadership. Notably, the future will undoubtedly bring new opportunities and challenges alike. You've probably noticed that successful teams leverage these capabilities to unlock unprecedented value. On one hand, flexibility improves satisfaction; on the other hand, isolation presents real constraints. The bottom line: we believe that with the right approach—which requires strategic planning, resource optimization, and continuous iteration—organizations can realize transformative outcomes in this dynamic digital age."

### After (Style-only revision, claims pending verification):
> "Remote work is fundamentally transforming how organizations support employee engagement. Research suggests that distributed teams enhance innovation and optimize operational efficiency, though one could argue that remote work presents unique challenges.
>
> Three critical factors emerge: streamlined communication, robust infrastructure, and adaptive leadership. The future will undoubtedly bring new opportunities and challenges. Successful teams use these capabilities to unlock unprecedented value. Flexibility improves satisfaction, while isolation presents real constraints.
>
> We believe that with the right approach, requiring strategic planning, resource optimization, and continuous iteration, organizations can achieve transformative outcomes in this dynamic digital age."

**Editorial note (not part of the revision):** Verify the research attribution, innovation and efficiency claims, scale of transformation and value, tradeoff, and confident prediction before publication. Broad framing remains where narrowing or deleting it would alter the argument. Ask for supported specifics rather than silently discarding claims. Add priorities or first-person experience only when the author supplies them.

### Key Changes Applied:
- ✓ Kept sourcing concerns outside the revised prose
- ✓ Simplified wording where meaning was retained; kept technical terms such as "resource optimization"
- ✓ Replaced interrupting em dashes with sentence boundaries and clauses
- ✓ Retained both sides of the tradeoff, the listed factors, and planning requirements
- ✓ Removed presumed shared experience without adding an opinion
- ✓ Retained the author's "we believe" and qualified claim rather than inventing a voice
- ✓ Preserved strong claims and certainty pending verification instead of silently weakening them
