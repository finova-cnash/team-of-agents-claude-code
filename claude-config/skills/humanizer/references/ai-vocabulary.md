# AI Vocabulary Reference

Words and phrases to review for formulaic writing. Clusters can prompt stylistic review, but they are not reliable proof of AI authorship. Preserve precise technical terms and source-backed claims rather than replacing words mechanically.

## Potentially Inflated Synonyms (Contextual Alternatives)

Use an alternative only when it preserves meaning, register, and technical precision. Words such as "optimize", "implement", and "procure" can carry domain-specific meanings that a generic substitute loses.

| Term to Review | Possible Alternative | Context |
|---------|-------------------|---------|
| utilize | use | "We utilize X" → "We use X" |
| leverage | use/apply | "Leverage your strengths" → "Use your strengths" |
| facilitate | help/enable | "Facilitate collaboration" → "Help teams collaborate" |
| enhance | improve/add | "Enhance performance" → "Improve performance" |
| optimize | improve/tune | Keep "optimize" for a defined optimization objective; otherwise consider "improve" |
| streamline | simplify | "Streamline workflows" → "Simplify workflows" |
| implement | carry out/put into practice | "Implement a strategy" → "Carry out a strategy"; keep technical implementation terms |
| demonstrate | show | "Demonstrates the point" → "Shows the point" |
| endeavor | try | "Our endeavor to..." → "We're trying to..." |
| paramount | most important/of greatest importance | "Paramount importance" → "Of greatest importance" |
| ascertain | find out/learn | "Ascertain the facts" → "Find out what happened" |
| alleviate | ease/reduce | "Alleviate concerns" → "Reduce concerns" |
| commence | start/begin | "Commence operations" → "Start operations" |
| peruse | read carefully | Preserve the intended depth of reading |
| procure | obtain/buy | Keep "procure" when it denotes a formal procurement process |

## Overused Conceptual Terms

Check whether these terms add precise meaning. Keep terminology consistent for the same concept rather than cycling synonyms:

- paradigm
- ecosystem
- landscape
- journey (metaphorical)
- narrative
- synergy
- holistic
- nuanced
- dynamic
- innovative
- transformative
- disruptive
- cutting-edge
- emerging
- evolving

Also review emphasis words such as crucial, fundamental, fascinating, compelling, and remarkable when they add importance without support.

## Vague Attribution Patterns

"Some say X" without specifics. Use supplied, verifiable sources or perspectives; otherwise flag the attribution instead of inventing evidence:

- Some argue that...
- It's widely believed...
- Research suggests...
- Experts agree...
- Many people...
- As we know...
- It's often said that...
- Conventional wisdom holds that...
- There's a saying that...
- Popular opinion suggests...
- General consensus is...
- Most people believe...

## Hedge Clusters

Review stacked qualifiers for redundancy, but keep distinctions in probability, scope, degree, or attribution:

- arguably somewhat potentially
- it could be argued that seemingly
- it might be said that ostensibly
- in a sense, one could argue
- to a certain extent, arguably
- it is often suggested that, arguably
- essentially, effectively, practically (when used as qualifiers rather than precise descriptions)

**Optional prompt:** Do the qualifiers repeat the same uncertainty, or express different limits? Remove only redundancy; do not strengthen a claim or add personal doubt.

## Filler Connectors

Review these in context; they may express a necessary logical relationship or emphasis rather than padding:

- notably
- interestingly
- importantly
- significantly
- furthermore
- moreover
- additionally
- consequently
- ultimately
- undoubtedly
- certainly
- clearly
- obviously
- unquestionably

**Optional prompt:** Would deleting the connector preserve the logical relationship, certainty, emphasis, and flow, not just grammatical completeness? Keep it when it signals a meaningful addition, contrast, consequence, or conclusion.

## False Precision Ranges

Review "anywhere from X to Y" when a verified approximate value would suffice. Keep genuine ranges and uncertainty intact; use a narrower estimate only if the source supports it. For example, "anywhere from 5 to 10 years" can become "5-10 years", not an unsupported "about 7 years".

**Fix:** Be specific where evidence permits; preserve existing uncertainty without adding doubt or false precision.

## Performed Casualness

Phrases that attempt to sound human but feel forced:

- spoiler: it's complicated
- (spoiler: it's complicated)
- (literally)
- honestly, (at the start of a sentence as filler)
- you'll want to...
- it turns out...
- what's interesting is...
- here's the thing...
- the truth is...
- the reality is...
- let me be clear...
- the bottom line is...

**Note:** These can be authentic. Clusters invite review in the author's voice and context; they do not establish performance or authorship.

## Quick Stylistic Screening

Optionally scan a passage in context for recurring patterns. No word counting, quotas, scores, or thresholds are required; this is a review aid, not a detector:

- **Word choices:** Are recurring formal or inflated terms precise and appropriate to the register?
- **Attributions:** Can the claims be traced to supplied, verifiable sources?
- **Punctuation:** Do repeated em dashes clarify relationships or disrupt the rhythm?
- **Contrasts:** Does the balance reflect the argument, or impose unsupported symmetry?
- **Groupings:** Do the listed items serve the content rather than a rhetorical template?
- **Qualifiers:** Do they preserve necessary uncertainty, or repeat the same hedge?

Consider clusters alongside purpose, genre, and voice. Neither the presence nor absence of these patterns establishes authorship; leave effective writing unchanged.
